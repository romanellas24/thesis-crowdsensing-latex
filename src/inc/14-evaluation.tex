\section{Gas cost analysis}\label{sec:gas-analysis}
There are three contracts under analysis: \texttt{PFC}, \texttt{PSC}, and \texttt{FIFO}.
\texttt{FIFO} shares the same base implementation as \texttt{PFC} but replaces the swap-and-pop pending list with a linear-shift FIFO approach.

The methodology of these experiments is described in Section~\ref{subsec:gas-analysis-methodology}.
Section~\ref{subsec:gas-pfc-vs-fifo} presents a comparison between the swap-and-pop data structure and FIFO queue.
Section~\ref{subsec:gas-pfc-vs-psc} contains a comparison to show the overhead introduced by the novel features, i.e., the cost of extending \texttt{PFC} to \texttt{PSC}.

Finally, Section~\ref{subsec:gas-real-world-costs} estimates the cost of organizing a crowdsensing campaign in USD.

\subsection{Methodology}\label{subsec:gas-analysis-methodology}
The test environment is based on Hardhat, the experiments are executed on a simulated Ethereum environment on the \texttt{cancun} hardfork was selected to avoid the per-transaction gas cap of $2^{24}$ gas introduced by EIP-7825~\cite{eip7825} in the Fusaka hard fork.
In addition, the gas limit per block is modified to $10^9$ to avoid interruption on worst-case.

A grid of scenarios, a grid made with a dimension of $4\times 4$, the scenarios are a combination between
\begin{gather*}
    \forall S \in \{1, 10, 100, 1000\}\\
    \forall V \in \{2, 10, 100, 1000\}\\
\end{gather*}
A grid of scenarios in Table~\ref{tab:proto-evaluation-scenario-grid}
\begin{table}
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
        \hline
        & $V = 2$ & $V = 10$ & $V = 100$ & $V = 1000$ \\
        \hline
        $S = 1$ & \shortstack{S: 1\\V: 2} & \shortstack{S: 1\\V: 10} & \shortstack{S: 1\\V: 100} & \shortstack{S: 1\\V: 1000} \\
        \hline
        $S = 10$ & \shortstack{S: 10\\V: 2} & \shortstack{S: 10\\V: 10} & \shortstack{S: 10\\V: 100} & \shortstack{S: 10\\V: 1000} \\

        $S = 100$ & \shortstack{S: 100\\V: 2} & \shortstack{S: 100\\V: 10} & \shortstack{S: 100\\V: 100} & \shortstack{S: 100\\V: 1000} \\
        \hline
        $S = 1000$ & \shortstack{S: 1000\\V: 2} & \shortstack{S: 1000\\V: 10} & \shortstack{S: 1000\\V: 100} & \shortstack{S: 1000\\V: 1000} \\

    \end{tabular}
    \caption{Simulation scenarios: 16 combinations of sender and verifier cardinalities.}
    \label{tab:proto-evaluation-scenario-grid}
\end{table}

Each scenario submits 10,000 CIDs. All participants are assumed to behave honestly, following the happy path of the protocol.

The results report gas units.
First call operations are highlighted because first calls initialize storage slots, incurring higher costs due to cold SSTORE operations.

\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{diagrams/proto-evaluation-pfc_vs_fifo_submit_overlay_1000s_1000v}
    \caption[Per-call gas of \texttt{verification\_submit}: PFC vs FIFO baseline.]{%
        Per-call gas consumption of \texttt{verification\_submit} in the
        $1000\,\text{S} \times 1000\,\text{V}$ scenario.
        The dashed line marks the EIP-7825 per-transaction gas cap
        of \(2^{24}\)\,gas, active from the Fusaka hard fork.
        The two low bands ($\approx 50\,\text{kgas}$) coincide for both
        contracts and correspond to submissions that do not trigger majority
        consensus. The high bands correspond to majority-triggering
        submissions. PFC stays bounded at $[150$, $280] \,\text{kgas}$ range,
        while FIFO traces a decreasing curve from $56\,\text{Mgas}$ down to
        $120\,\text{kgas}$ as the pending list shrinks.}
    \label{fig:proto-evaluation-pfc-vs-fifo-submit-overlay}
\end{figure}

\texttt{PrivacyFirstContract} applies swap-and-pop approach to \texttt{\_removeFromPending} function.
The deletion is made in time $O(1)$, but the order is not preserved.

In this context, there is the need to implement a new Smart Contract that implements a FIFO pending data to verify queue, implemented as shown in Listing~\ref{lst:chap-c-fifo-contract}.
This smart contract is a variant that inherit all the functions from \texttt{PFC} and overrides the functions \texttt{\_addToPending} and \texttt{\_removeFromPending} to implement a FIFO strategy.
